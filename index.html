Welcome to Physique Lab

Everything here is measurement-driven — no guessed activity levels, no gender fudge-factors. It now covers body composition, nutrition tracking, and training programming in one place, organized up top into Body, Nutrition, Training, and Progress. Follow these steps in order the first time through; after that, jump to any group whenever you like.

Tab Position

The Measurements tab defaults to right after Start Here within the Body group. Once your stats are entered, you might not need it up front anymore — move it to the end and everything else in Body slides forward. Your inputs are unaffected either way.

A Few Things Worth Knowing

  • Fields marked optional aren't required for the core calculations — but several tabs (Genetic Potential, Comparisons, the Visualizer) only unlock fully once you fill them in.
  • Everything you enter saves automatically to this device and reloads next time you open the app. Nothing is uploaded anywhere — the food database, exercise list, and everything else all work fully offline.
  • In Training, once your program is built the way you want, the "Ready to Train?" toggle on the Program tab makes Live Session the default instead, so you're not tapping past the builder every time you just want to log a workout.
  • Your selected coach style (Program tab) drives auto-progression everywhere — including inside Live Session's exercise-swap and the Sandbox's one-off lifts — and it respects the equipment increments you set, so suggestions round to weight you can actually load.
  • The Progress tab is where the app becomes genuinely useful over time — a single visit only tells you where you are, not where you're headed.

Adonis / Golden Ratio Index

Chest-to-waist ratio. Classical ideal ≈ 1.618 : 1.

–
Your Ratio
–
Rating

The ratio moves from two directions — widening the top and narrowing the waist — and they respond to different things:

  • Widen the top: lateral delts (lateral raises, overhead press, upright rows) drive shoulder width directly. Lat width (pull-ups/chin-ups, lat pulldowns, rows) adds visual taper from the back and is often the single biggest lever for this ratio.
  • Narrow the waist: waist size is driven almost entirely by body fat, not ab training — sit-ups and crunches don't shrink it. A fat-loss phase (calorie deficit) is what actually moves this number.
  • Avoid: heavy weighted side bends or loaded oblique twists. They can add oblique mass and thicken the waist, working directly against the ratio.

Worth knowing: clavicle width and rib cage size are fixed by skeletal structure, so 1.618 exactly isn't achievable for everyone — but meaningful improvement toward it almost always is.

Hardgainer / Easygainer Classification

Based on wrist-to-height and ankle-to-height ratios from Casey Butt's data set (avg. wrist ≈10.45% of height, ankle ≈12.96% of height).

–
Upper Body (Wrist)
–
Lower Body (Ankle)

A thin wrist or ankle relative to height means a smaller skeletal frame — less bone and joint structure for muscle to attach to and wrap around, independent of how hard you train. This is what "hardgainer" actually refers to: not a metabolism problem, a frame-size fact. Practically, it usually means:

  • Hardgainer (narrow frame): a lower absolute ceiling on muscle mass and slower visible progress at the same training age. Often responds better to slightly higher calorie surpluses, fewer but harder working sets, and more recovery time between sessions than generic "bro split" volume recommendations assume.
  • Easygainer (wide frame): a higher absolute ceiling — more bone and joint structure to build onto — but this cuts both ways: fat tends to add just as readily as muscle, so body composition (not just weight) needs closer attention during a surplus.

Neither classification changes how hard you can train or how disciplined your nutrition can be — it just sets the ceiling and the pace. A hardgainer who trains and eats consistently for years will still out-build an easygainer who doesn't train at all.

One-Rep Max Estimator

Epley formula: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

–
Estimated 1RM
This formula is built for major compound barbell lifts — bench press, back squat, deadlift, overhead press, barbell row — done in roughly the 1-10 rep range. Accuracy drops outside that: above ~10-12 reps the estimate runs too high (fatigue and technique breakdown matter more than raw strength at that point), and it's not meaningful for machines/cables (the machine's own leverage changes the numbers), stability-heavy lifts (single-arm or single-leg work), or technical Olympic lifts (clean, snatch) where skill limits the number as much as strength does.

Backup & Restore

Everything (inputs, snapshots, lift log, food log, recipes) lives only in this browser on this device — nothing is uploaded anywhere. That means clearing site data or switching devices loses it unless you've saved a backup file somewhere safe first.

On a phone, this opens your normal share sheet where you can send it to yourself (Messages, Mail, Files/Drive, AirDrop) — that's usually the easiest way to get it off this one device. On desktop, if your browser supports it, you'll get to pick exactly which folder to save it in. Every backup uses the same file name (Physique_Lab_backup.json) so saving to the same spot each time replaces the old one — though if your browser just does a plain download instead (no picker, no share sheet), it may add "(1)" to the name rather than truly overwriting, which is a browser setting, not something this app controls.

When this is on, a backup file saves itself automatically right after you finish a workout, and once a day if you've logged food that day — same idea as an autosave checkpoint in a game. You can still save manually anytime above regardless of this setting.

Starting fresh, or clearing out test data? This wipes every input, snapshot, and logged lift on this device. Export a backup first if there's anything worth keeping.

General Rules

  • Use a flexible cloth/vinyl tape, not a metal one — it needs to follow the curve of the body.
  • Snug against the skin, flat and level — not compressing it. If the tape leaves a mark, it's too tight.
  • Take every measurement 2-3 times and use the average. Small angle changes shift readings more than you'd expect.
  • Measure at the same time of day each time (mornings, before eating/drinking, after the bathroom) — waist especially can swing an inch over a day.
  • Stand relaxed in a natural posture unless a step below says to flex. Don't suck in your stomach or puff up your chest.
